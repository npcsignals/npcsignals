# terrorism statistics in europe: what are we actually measuring?

npcsignals · 2015–2025

[method and scope](../../METHODOLOGY.md) · [source guide](../phase25/SOURCE_GUIDE.md) · [figure text alternatives](TEXT_ALTERNATIVES.md). the study uses eleven annual reporting years. all attack counts describe entries in those statistical reports.

## what changes when the geography is held fixed?

on the fixed geography of the current 27 eu member states, the comparison is **55 reported attack entries in 2019 and 57 in 2020**. the original 2019 total was 119, including 64 uk entries. a later europol edition gives 56 for 2020. national classification and reporting practices can still differ. [c01–c03, c05]

## when the reporting year is not the occurrence year

the original fixed-eu27 reporting series records 28 attack entries in 2022 and 120 in 2023: an increase of 92 entries. however, the 2023 reporting cohort includes 26 french separatist entries referring to earlier occurrence years, four assigned to 2021 and 22 assigned to 2022. [c04, c06]

the investigation therefore keeps two distinct representations. one preserves the original annual reporting cohorts. the other is a **conditional sensitivity calculation** that also incorporates later official adjustments: 2020 changes from 57 to 56 and 2021 from 15 to 18. the latter revision adds three french foiled right-wing entries, separate from the 26 earlier french separatist entries. when the 26 french entries are conditionally allocated to their stated occurrence years, the scenario reads 56 in 2020, 22 in 2021, 50 in 2022 and 94 in 2023. the increase from 2022 to 2023 is then 44 rather than 92. [c05–c06]

this illustrates how strongly a year-to-year comparison can depend on the reporting cohort. it is **not** a new official occurrence-year series. individual event matches and possible overlap remain unresolved. the scenario cannot establish the number of physical terrorist incidents that occurred in each year.

## similar terms, different counting units

the reviewed public records do not provide a single keyed european event roster that permits every incident to be followed across the relevant institutions.

for 2025, north rhine-westphalia describes five jihadist-motivated attacks in germany. a federal source names four locations, while europol reports four completed german jihadist attack entries. in france, the national counterterrorism prosecution authority, pnat, describes three completed jihadist-inspired attacks, while europol reports two completed french jihadist entries. [c14–c15]

the sources describe different counting units and categories. without a shared event identifier, the reason for each difference remains unresolved. they do **not**, on their own, identify a missing event, a reporting error or a specific transfer between institutions.

the same caution applies to arrests and judicial decisions. fixed-eu27 reported arrest counts are 943 in 2015, 380 in 2022 and 486 in 2025. for reference year 2025, TE-SAT 2026 reports 406 convictions and 99 acquittals in concluded proceedings submitted to eurojust by 21 member states. some decisions were still subject to appeal. these are not the same identified population as the people arrested during 2025. no conviction-to-arrest conversion rate can be inferred. [c07–c08]

## what can we identify behind the statistical entries?

the investigation also attempted to link statistical entries to identifiable incidents using available sources.

across 1,129 entries in the original annual reporting cohorts, 168 were adequately identified, 35 were candidate entries and **926 remained unidentified by this project's public-source linkage**. this denominator includes 410 uk entries through 2019. excluding those country rows and their confidence counts leaves **719 fixed EU27 entries: 156 adequately identified, 33 candidates and 530 unidentified**. both totals retain the respective original reporting groups. [reproducing the fixed geography breakdown](TEXT_ALTERNATIVES.md#fixed-eu27-identification-breakdown). for 2025, the respective figures are seven adequately identified, 17 candidates and 21 unidentified out of 45. [c09–c10]

an adequate match has enough documentary evidence to link a named case to a statistical entry. a candidate has a plausible match with a remaining evidentiary gap. an unidentified entry lacks a usable public match in this crosswalk. the original 2021 report names cases accounting for 14 of its 15 entries; the remaining german entry is a candidate. later additions are outside that original denominator. the published records cannot quantify how much documentation, category mix or method accounts for differences across years.

these figures measure **this investigation's ability to link published statistical entries to available source material**. they do not establish that europol lacks internal documentation for 926 incidents, or that those incidents did not occur. a single statistical entry may have several competing event candidates. in 2025, 18 possible identities relate to 17 candidate entries.

## what a judicial decision actually establishes

three judicial examples illustrate why initial classification, investigation, allegations and court findings must be kept separate.

in the berlin city motorway case (A100), the regional court's notice reports that the judge rejected the initial islamist attack characterization and ordered psychiatric placement after finding the accused lacked criminal responsibility. the decision became final following review on 16 february 2023. in a separate statistical record, TE-SAT 2023 p. 9 n. 4 says one german entry was no longer counted following a court ruling. the note supplies no event identifier, and A100 remains a candidate match. [c11]

in the holbæk case in denmark, the [eastern high court's announcement, case S-25-23](https://domstoldk.euwest01.umbraco.io/oestrelandsret/aktuelt/2024/5/faengsel-i-16-aar-og-udvisning-for-bla-forsoeg-paa-terrorisme/) records a conviction on appeal and a 16 year sentence on 14 may 2024. the offences included attempted terrorism and terrorist financing. PET also reported the ruling in an agency statement. later review or finality beyond that appellate decision remains unverified. [c12]

in the solingen case, the federal court of justice dismissed the appeal on **12 may 2026**; the announcement was published on **22 may**. it summarizes lower-court findings about encouragement and advice from is contacts. that finding is not equivalent to verified operational command by those contacts. [c13]

the three cases illustrate different evidentiary boundaries. they are not a representative sample and do not establish a common command structure.

## discrepancies that remain unresolved

some published observations cannot yet be reconciled using the available sources:

- **2015:** TE-SAT 2016 reports **211** in its original annual data (pp. 10–11, 44). the later TE-SAT 2017 edition repeats **211** in the prose (p. 10) but plots **193** for 2015 in figure 1 (p. 11). the discrepancy's cause is unknown.
- **2016:** europol reports 379 injured overall, while three incident narratives add to 597. its annex gives 374 for jihadist casualties, using the annex’s own terminology. the sources do not provide a common measurement definition that resolves the difference.
- **2023:** the original edition and a later overview report 426 arrests, while retrospective category tables sum to 431.
- **2025:** the table reports 347 jihadist arrests, while an age footnote uses 344.
- **corsica, october 2023:** the french interior ministry records 32 facts in two police jurisdictions, while europol describes 34 attacks for the overnight series. the sources use different terms and lack a common event register. the reason the totals differ remains unresolved. [c16–c18]

these differences remain visible because they affect interpretation and potential calculations. they do not, by themselves, establish negligence, manipulation or misconduct.

## what can we conclude?

interpreting europol's terrorism statistics requires at least three questions: **which countries are included, which reporting year contains an entry, and what does each institution count?**

many published totals can be checked, and the consequences of fixed geography and different reporting cohorts can be shown. but the current evidence does not support a complete census of physical terrorist incidents across the project’s research area of the 27 current eu members, the uk, norway, iceland and liechtenstein or a single trend in actual attack frequency. nor does it establish comparable longitudinal series for unique individuals, injuries, financing or operational command. [c19–c20]

**the figures measure entries reported under each edition’s geography and counting rules. changes in those entries alone cannot establish changes in physical attack frequency.**
