# sources and source notes

Source documents are linked rather than bundled. The private research archive preserves copies and hashes where permitted; redistribution rights were not established for the original PDFs and report ZIPs.

## platform and ranking

- TikTok, [Digital Services Act Risk Assessment and Mitigation Report 2025](https://sf16-va.tiktokcdn.com/obj/eden-va2/zayvwlY_fjulyhwzuhy%5B/ljhwZthlaukjlkulzlp/DSA/DSARiskAssessmentReport-TikTokTechnologyLimited-2025.pdf). Company statement; safeguards described are not independent effectiveness evidence.
- TikTok, [How TikTok recommends content](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/exploring-videos/how-tiktok-recommends-content). Provider explanation of broad signals and feed rules.
- TikTok Research, [Research API codebook](https://developers.tiktok.com/docs/en/research-api-codebook) and [Research Tools](https://developers.tiktok.com/products/research-api/). Provider documentation; not a complete individual exposure log.
- Baumann et al., [Dynamics of algorithmic content amplification on TikTok](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjds/s13688-026-00629-2). Published controlled account study; the figures cited in chapter 2 are source-reported and have not been recomputed from raw data.
- ByteDance authors, [watch-time debiasing paper](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v40i18.38555). Publication does not establish that this method is deployed in TikTok’s European service.

## user controls and political exposure

- Kaplan et al., [When ‘For You’ Isn’t For You: Measuring User Agency in TikTok’s Algorithmic Feed](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10690), arXiv v1, 11 May 2026. Full paper and methods checked; preprint, no independent reproduction.
- Tjaden et al., [Does the TikTok Feed Lean Right?](https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2026.2733320), *Political Communication*, 2026. Final article and online supplementary descriptions checked. Its earlier preprint is the same research project, not independent confirmation.
- Wedel et al., [Does Your Feed Match How You Vote?](https://doi.org/10.34669/wi.wjds/7.2.1), *Weizenbaum Journal of the Digital Society*, published 19 November 2026. Full paper, sample limitations, data and funding statements checked; raw user-level data not public and code not rerun.
- Ibrahim et al., [Systematic partisan content skews in TikTok during the 2024 US elections](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10447-1). Automated-account study; US election context only.
- Hagar & Diakopoulos, [proactive news distribution audit](https://doi.org/10.1177/14614448231192964). Abstract-level use only; historical US setting.

## perception and political attitudes

- [Randomized Bluesky ranking experiment](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10536-1). Cross-platform counterfinding; abstract and research briefing checked, not TikTok evidence.

- Brady et al., [Overperception of moral outrage in online social networks inflates beliefs about intergroup hostility](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-023-01582-0). Bibliographic record and abstract checked; full article could not be retrieved in this pass.
- Lerman, Yan & Wu, [The “Majority Illusion” in Social Networks](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0147617). Model-based network result, not a TikTok measurement.
- Gauthier et al., [The political effects of X’s feed algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10098-2). United States X experiment; not a TikTok replication.
- Liu et al., [political attitudes and video recommendations](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2318127122). Custom YouTube-like experiments; short-term and platform-specific.
- Guess et al., [Science article](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abp9364), [2024 erratum](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adu8261), and [2026 erratum](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aeh2575). Correction metadata confirmed; full correction texts were not accessible, so detailed estimates are not used as public support.

## source-use and verification policy

“Confirmed” in the evidence register means that the source or archived file explicitly records the stated fact. It does not automatically confirm a study’s causal interpretation. “Supported” means the result is reported in a publication or source, with the verification level stated. No claim in this archive is described as independently empirically replicated. Citations, full-text access, supplement review, data/code reproduction and replication are different verification levels.
