# the architecture of influence

## how systems shape what we see, what we perceive and what we know.

A feed feels like a window onto the world. It is also a selection. Platforms decide what is eligible to appear, rank eligible material, and learn from activity. Creators, advertisers, user choices, languages and events shape the material available to the system. This investigation asks what can be established about those layers in TikTok’s European service and where public evidence stops.

### what the evidence supports

- TikTok describes controls intended to diversify and shape the For You feed. A company description establishes what the company says it does; it does not establish how well a safeguard works.
- Controlled audits and German election studies report that accounts and people can encounter personalized and political content. They measure different things. Neither by itself isolates the effect of ranking from the supply of videos, user behavior or creator networks.
- A mobile-app audit found that negative feedback could reduce topic exposure in tested accounts. Its reported “relapse” followed the accounts returning to positive viewing of the topic. It did not test a spontaneous return without new positive signals.
- A German user-history study found political-content exposure and alignment with reported vote choice in a self-selected sample. It does not establish that the recommender caused that alignment.
- A correction to TikTok’s transparency report changed two category rows and exposed duplicated CSV headers. The archive shows differences in the reports; it does not establish why the correction occurred or that notices moved from one category to another.
- European oversight includes binding commitments and preliminary findings. Preliminary findings are not final infringement decisions. Norway’s DSA implementation position is separate from the EU’s enforcement arrangement.

### what this investigation does not establish

It does not reverse-engineer TikTok’s production ranking weights, prove intentional political manipulation, show that exposure changes persuasion or voting, or establish that every platform safeguard fails. In several areas, the best supported result is a bounded observation, a counterfinding, or an unresolved question.

**Behind the words:** *exposure* means content that appeared or was recorded as watched; it does not mean persuasion. *Ranking* means ordering items that are available and eligible; it does not mean the platform removed all alternatives. *A preliminary finding* is the regulator’s view at a stage when the provider may respond; it is not a final decision.
